# Shared Metro pursuit architecture

The only plant is `World` on Metro v4: City and headless/Lab submit normalized `{steer, throttle, brake, gear}` to the same fixed-step pursuit entrypoint. The shared swept solver owns accepted poses and contacts. Alternatives rejected: another map/runtime (dynamics and content drift); extending the privileged GTA vector for the learned policy (hidden actor state leakage); replacing archived rule behavior in place (replay breakage).

## M1 interface: shared dispatch, physical pressure and observable sensing

* Old `variant: "gta"` start commands without `config.ruleset` continue `legacy` behavior, including archived terminal timing, velocity and the 131-field privileged `GTA_OBSERVATION_SCHEMA`. New starts explicitly record `ruleset: "dispatch-v1"` and the resolved settings where appropriate. Existing browser GTA entry, Arcade and Tactical remain selectable; the new manual dispatch selection does not imply a trained model.
* `surveillanceZones` is a validated list (at most 16) of uniquely identified inclusive `{id,minX,maxX,minZ,maxZ}` rectangles in world metres. The three default rectangles cover interlinked road districts within Metro's approximately 332 m by 202 m road extents, with a reachable uncovered perimeter. Initial design: civic `[-256,-156] x [-120,36]`, gateway `[-174,-70] x [-118,18]`, market `[-102,25] x [-68,23]`. No global tracking toggle or invisible world-state report during loss.
* Pursuit response retains `sightedBy`/`closePatrols` for local observations. `response.dispatch` is versioned runtime state; `state` is `ZONE_TRACKED`, `VISUALLY_SPOTTED`, `LAST_KNOWN`, `SEARCHING`, `LOST`, or `ESCAPED`. `lastSeen` is a pose/velocity report sampled only from an active zone or actual local sight. A report from one unit is shared. Local tactical contact still requires local sight; location-sensitive reinforcements require a current report. During loss no steering or dispatch target reads current hidden ego position. Ground-truth distance may be used for objective scoring, never police route targeting.
* Escape needs at least 1 s of valid tracking with a patrol within 65 m actually travelling at 3 m/s or more, then at least 45 m from chase origin, 32 m from the last report and 28 m clear of every active patrol, followed by uninterrupted loss and wanted decay. Inside a zone or on reacquisition the decay clock resets. Disabled, immobile or artificially slow units cannot earn escape eligibility. Search state and police targets only use time and the last report; separation checks are for terminal scoring, not target selection.
* Default Dispatch scoring (`qualified-progress-v2`) starts displacement credit only when the chase qualifies. The watermark still advances beforehand, so no past movement earns retroactive credit and repeated ground pays nothing. The safety cost includes real non-ego contacts involving a civilian vehicle or pedestrian, once per incident, with a higher pedestrian cost. Patrol-only and patrol-to-static contacts do not incur that civilian cost. This changes the training signal, not motion, visible observations, or replay. No-patrol curriculum drills explicitly select the unchanged `maneuver-practice-v1`; earlier checkpoints can still resume, but scores across reward profiles are not directly comparable.
* New capable units target straight-line advantage approximately 10–25%; heavy units need not have that cap. The physical verdict uses accepted velocity after acceleration on a real road, not profile configuration. Dynamics and basic collision handling remain shared.
* `PursuitScanner.sample(world)` is a chase-local observation adapter. It runs after accepted poses at tick 0 and every six fixed ticks (10 Hz); consumers reuse the frame until the next sample. The immutable frame is `{tick, rays, tracks}`: 72 uniform ego-local first-return rays (5 degrees, 48 m), measured distance and class only. Free only up to the return/range; no extrapolated free space behind a return. Classes are `none`, `static`, `civilian`, `pedestrian`, `police`. Tracks are surface measurements, not actor centers; fields `{kind,rightM,forwardM,rightMps,forwardMps,ageS,motionKnown,ttcS}` use relative velocity from repeated returns and `null` TTC when unknown. Stale tracks expire after 0.5 s, are marked by positive age and unknown motion, and never acquire new pose information while hidden. Thin shapes can fall between rays and the range is finite. This is virtual planar geometry, **not** RGB, lidar hardware or real perception.
* Dispatch Env emits a separately named finite flat vector with schema/version and clipped scales. The current allowlist is own speed/steer/slip/yaw/health, frame age and first-return geometry/tracks. The optional route schema also supplies static-map cues in the ego frame. Wanted decay depends on hidden separation, so neither version exposes wanted stars or police reports, hidden actor poses, capture/cooldown clocks, ground-truth contact flags or privileged FSM. `info` and product debug snapshots may contain labeled reference metrics, and are not learner inputs. No automatic fallback to the privileged vector. Invisible road boundaries are not geometry returns; the plant's road-containment guard still applies.
* Dispatch opens with unobstructed physical berths on Metro. The first tail prefers a heading-aligned rear berth or a connected approach into the road behind the SUV. At four and five stars, opposing same-street candidates must leave a four-second reaction distance using each vehicle's acceleration and speed cap. The test only chooses berths; it does not slow patrols during the chase. Two positions are reserved for a roadblock during the first 12 seconds when permitted. A new blockade needs a fresh authorized report, conservative whole-footprint concealment, and a drivable turn before the site using its current physical occupancy. This is a placement-time alternative, not a guarantee of later clearance.
* `dispatch-route-v1` preserves the old public-map targets. `dispatch-route-v2` uses reachable endpoints that leave room beyond the public coverage rectangles and carries the same nine features with distinct schema/checkpoint identity. Neither receives the current surveillance report, temporary block or any hidden separation metric. A valid static continuation is not evidence that the learned vehicle will take it.
* Both Lab and City use the same brake guard: only fresh stationary civilian surfaces measured across consecutive scans may qualify for a low-speed forward stopping sweep. The guard checks the requested steer; moving/uncertain hazards and pedestrian yielding are not waived. Dispatch patrols independently brake for locally visible pedestrians projected into their stopping path. No NPC observations are exposed to the escaping policy.
* `civilianCrossingPreview` is a recorded Dispatch-only development option. `true` preserves the first preview, which checks the motion of walkers with the authored crossing flag. `"visible-motion-v2"` also checks moving walkers without that flag when the static line of sight is clear. Both project the walker into the car's stopping path before yielding; neither supplies NPC state to the learned driver. The public City and current training manifests leave the option off. The first preview hurt learned escape retention, and the second has only local safety evidence, not full transfer.
* `patrolTrafficPreview: "visible-traffic-v1"` is a separate recorded development option, off by default. A patrol can brake for a civilian vehicle in its projected path only when the static line of sight is clear. The preview uses bounded constant-velocity extrapolation, tries a supported stopping steer and leaves final motion to the unchanged contact solver. It excludes the SUV and other patrols; it does not add information to the learned policy. Local contact and stop/resume controls passed, but full-City escape retention failed.

## Dependency order after M1

1. M2: strengthen route/role outcomes, record the scenario matrix and negative controls; pin observation/action and collision/reward contracts including shield behavior. Separate development validation seeds from unknown holdout seeds and freeze before optimization.
2. M3: collect real transitions and learn. Choose one continuous-control algorithm with resumable optimizer and seed state; first prove updates and frozen inference on a bounded CPU run, then run justified curricula/validation. Preserve previous weights and failed attempts.
3. M4: wire frozen policy and data provenance into the existing City and Lab UI, with explicit manual takeover and failed/missing model state; tune presentation against measured runtime.
4. M5: untouched holdout, simultaneous safety/escape rates, all L01–L20, actual browser journeys, density/frame metrics, replay and independent QA. Full source fingerprints and original-root acceptance only after all parent gates pass.
